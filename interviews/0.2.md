## Q1: Why did the health check's `DOWN` transition take noticeable time to appear, rather than being instant?

**Answer given (first pass):** "It waits for a timeout."

**Why this was too vague:** true, but doesn't name a mechanism — wouldn't survive a follow-up question asking *which* timeout, or why.

**Accepted answer:** Spring Boot Actuator's health check is pull-based — there's no background polling constantly checking the database. When you hit `/actuator/health`, it tries the connection right then. A stopped container's closed port fails fast at the OS level (a connection refused is quick). The actual delay comes from HikariCP's own connection pool bookkeeping: discovering that a pooled *idle* connection is now dead, evicting it, and opening a replacement — that eviction/validation cycle is what takes the visible time, not a network timeout.

## Q2: Does an `UP` health status guarantee the next real request will succeed?

**Answer given (first pass):** No — correct conclusion, but justified with a scattershot list of unrelated causes (memory leak, network issues, credential expiry) rather than one reasoned mechanism.

**Accepted answer:** No, and the general reason is a TOCTOU (time-of-check-to-time-of-use) gap: the health check is a snapshot at time T. Anything that changes the system's state between T and the next real request — pool exhaustion, a connection dying, a burst of concurrent load — can make that next request fail even though the last health check reported healthy. It's not any single named cause; it's the general shape of "a snapshot doesn't bind the future."

## Q3: What would the resource cost of Option A (one Postgres container per service) look like at 8 services instead of 2?

**Answer given (first pass):** "CPU/memory/storage" — framed as a resource-footprint problem.

**Why this was wrong:** ADR-0003's own arithmetic for 2 containers put combined overhead at roughly 50–100MB against 24GB of available RAM — noise. That ratio doesn't flip direction at 8 containers either (roughly 200–400MB) — still noise.

**Accepted answer:** The real cost at scale isn't CPU/memory/storage — it's operational bookkeeping: port allocation across 8 services, `docker-compose.yml` duplication and typo risk, and simply remembering what's running where. The mitigation is Compose YAML anchors/extension fields plus a documented port convention — not consolidating containers, which would just reintroduce the isolation loss Option B was already rejected for.
