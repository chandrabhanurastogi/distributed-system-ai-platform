## Q1: Why doesn't Spring Boot's `@AutoConfiguration` mechanism depend on which package a class lives in, the way component scanning does?

**Answer given (first pass):** "It makes it part of Spring scanning from the parent module" — a real conceptual error, treating auto-configuration as an extension of component scanning rather than a separate mechanism.

**Accepted answer (reached across two attempts):** Component scanning and auto-configuration are two different discovery mechanisms, not one extending the other. Component scanning discovers classes by walking a package tree starting from the `@SpringBootApplication` class's own package — that's why a class outside that tree is invisible to it. Auto-configuration works completely differently: it loads classes by their explicit, fully-qualified name, listed in a `META-INF/spring/...AutoConfiguration.imports` manifest file. Since there's no tree being walked at all, there's no package boundary to be blocked by — the manifest just says "load this exact class," regardless of where it lives.

## Q2: What exactly does `@ConditionalOnMissingBean` check, and what happens to the auto-configured bean when a user-defined bean is present?

**Answer given:** Reached the right outcome (a user-defined bean takes precedence) but with two imprecisions.

**Imprecision 1:** initially described the check as being by "name or type." **Correction:** the check is by *type* only, inferred from the annotated bean method's declared return type — not by bean name.

**Imprecision 2:** initially described the auto-configured bean as being "overridden" once the user's bean is found. **Correction:** the auto-configured bean is never instantiated in the first place when the condition fails — its constructor never runs, its side effects never happen. It isn't created-then-replaced; it's simply never created. This distinction matters for reasoning about any constructor side effects (logging, registration, resource acquisition) the auto-configured bean's constructor might otherwise have had.
