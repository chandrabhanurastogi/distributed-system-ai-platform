Network Interchange and Scheme-Rule Reference

Each card network reserves specific bank identification number ranges for its issuers. The first six to eight digits of a card number identify both network and issuer. A processor uses this range to route authorization automatically.

Interchange rates follow qualification tiers reflecting transaction risk. Card-present, chip-verified transactions qualify for the lowest tier. A transaction failing documentation is downgraded to a higher tier at settlement.

A cross-border fee applies whenever issuer and acquirer sit in different countries. This holds regardless of the transaction's settlement currency. The fee is a percentage charged on top of interchange.

Dynamic currency conversion lets a merchant quote the cardholder's home currency at checkout. A cardholder may decline and settle in the merchant's local currency instead. Failing to disclose the rate before authorization violates network rules.

Merchant category codes assign a risk tier based on historical chargeback rates. A high-risk code carries additional reserve requirements at the acquirer. A miscoded merchant may have its code corrected retroactively.

An acquirer may fee a merchant that misses its required PCI DSS validation window. This fee recurs monthly until a valid attestation is filed. It is assessed independently of any breach liability the merchant separately incurs.

A merchant filing representment has forty-five days to escalate to pre-arbitration. A further thirty days are allowed to escalate again, to binding arbitration. Missing either window forfeits the right to keep disputing that chargeback.

Representment evidence must directly rebut the specific reason code that was filed. Generic delivery proof alone does not rebut a not-as-described chargeback. Evidence addressing the wrong reason code risks outright rejection.

Both issuer and acquirer pay a pre-arbitration filing fee once a dispute escalates. This fee is non-refundable regardless of who prevails at arbitration. The losing party also bears the network's arbitration case fee.
